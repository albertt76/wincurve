NBA projected records

Built from each team's roster as it stood before the season began: every player's impact per 100 possessions is projected from his prior history with age curves applied, weighted by expected minutes, then the season is simulated game by game on a real schedule, with a carryover adjustment for the part of last season's error that tends to persist.

On the what-if tool. Roster edits are computed the same way the model computes a team, but the model was validated on whole rosters, not on single-player swaps. The impact-to-rating slope is fitted to map a team's aggregate talent onto its rating, and it absorbs some compression in the underlying player metric, so one-player counterfactuals extrapolate it further than the backtest checked. Treat the direction and rough scale as meaningful and the exact number as indicative. When you remove a player his minutes are offered to teammates who can cover his position, capped by how far each can stretch beyond his established role — so a deep roster absorbs the loss far better than a thin one, and only what the bench cannot cover falls to replacement level.

What every number means — plain-English glossary
Impact
Projected net points per 100 possessions a player adds versus an average player — a rate, not a win count. About 0 is an average rotation player, +4 a star (SGA is +4.0), −1.1 is replacement level (a freely-available end-of-bench body). It already contains his scoring, rebounding, and defense, so "fit" is not added on top.
Off / Def
Impact split into its offensive and defensive halves (they add up to total impact), projected and calibrated separately. Each team row shows the team total as off / def, so you can see whether a projection is carried or dragged by one side. Defense is the model's weak spot: it is built from box-score stats that are ~60% rebounds and blocks, so it mostly rewards rim protection and rebounding and underrates perimeter defenders.
D↑ / D↓
A defender our box-score metric probably misjudges, flagged by comparing it to RAPM (regularized adjusted plus/minus — a play-by-play defensive measure that sees perimeter defense the box score cannot). D↑ = RAPM rates his defense higher, so we likely underrate him; D↓ = RAPM lower, so we likely overrate him. Hover for both numbers. Comparison only — RAPM is not yet in the projection (its aggregate gain is absorbed by the carryover; it helps most on high-turnover rosters).
🛡1 ★2
Eye-test honors, from the player's recent selections (superscript = team, 1–3). 🛡 = All-Defensive Team, ★ = All-NBA; hover for how many times and when. These are shown, not scored — walk-forward, prior-year honors did not beat the backtest (a team's defense is already the sum of its players' countable events, so the honor adds nothing at the team level, and All-NBA is largely an offensive award). They earn their place as context: a shield next to a low Def is the eye test flagging a perimeter stopper the box score misses.
back
An injury-return override (upcoming season only). A star who missed most or all of last season is otherwise mis-projected — his minutes fall back to a bench default and the availability model marks him down — so he is projected as his last healthy season: pre-injury offense/defense, that season's minutes, at a hand-set expected availability (eased-in where noted). Hover for the specific reason. These are manual, forward-looking judgment calls applied to this projection only; they never touch the backtest (which uses real historical rosters). Note the metric restores what it thought of the player when healthy — which for some names (e.g. Fred VanVleet) is below average, so the fix is mostly to his minutes, not a boost.
← LAL / Offseason moves
An offseason arrival (this player was on another team — here LAL — last season). The Offseason moves panel inside each team lists its arrivals and departures; clicking one undoes the move (an arrival goes back, a departure returns) and reprices both teams through the same what-if recompute, so you can read how much a move changed each side’s projection. This is roster movement — trades, free agency and waivers combined; the transaction type isn’t in any free data feed, so it is not labelled “trades” only.
≈ Wins
The WAR-like (wins-above-replacement) translation of a player's value: how many of the team's wins he accounts for versus a replacement-level player taking his minutes. It depends on impact and minutes — a +1.3 star playing 32 minutes is worth far more than a +2.4 reserve playing 18 — and it decomposes the same model the team rating uses: offense and defense priced by their own slopes (defense's is lower), with defense blended toward RAPM by roster turnover. So a rebounding centre the box score over-credits on defense is discounted here, not treated as a star. The small o / d line beneath each ≈Wins splits it into the offensive and defensive wins it is built from (they add to the total), so you can see whether a player's value is carried by offense or defense — a two-way wing shows both positive, a scoring guard often shows a negative d.
Wins (big number)
The average of the simulated season — our single best guess. Trust the range around it more than the exact figure.
80% / 50% range
The bars: the wide bar is the 80% range (4 seasons out of 5 the real result should land inside), the solid inner bar the 50% range. Teal = steady roster, amber = high turnover and a wider, less certain range.
Range width
Half the 80% range, in wins — a one-number summary of how uncertain a team's season is.
green / red ±
Appears only after you edit a roster: the change in projected wins caused by your edit, versus this team's original projection. Removing a good player makes it negative.
rapm (RAPM-only arm)
The small triangle on the bar and the rapm readout: a second win projection with defense priced purely from play-by-play RAPM (regularized adjusted plus/minus) instead of the shipped box+RAPM blend, all else equal. The signed number is RAPM-only minus the blend — positive means RAPM rates this team's defense higher than the box score does, a perimeter signal the box misses. The gap is a defensive-uncertainty flag; it is largest on low-turnover teams (where the blend leans on the box metric) and smallest on high-turnover ones (where the blend already leans on RAPM). Display only — re-weighting the blend toward RAPM did not beat the backtest, so the projection keeps the turnover blend.
market
The hollow ring on the bar and the market readout: the win total the market implies. For the upcoming season it is the live Kalshi line; for a completed season it is that year's preseason Vegas over/under (bbref), set before tip-off, which you can now grade against the actual diamond on the same bar. The signed number is our projection minus the market. Comparison only — the market never feeds the model. Season-by-season, who was closer is in the Track record view.
MPG / Avail
MPG = minutes per game from last season's role. Avail = projected share of the season the player is available, from his games-missed history and age.
Minutes supplied
Inside a team: the active roster's total minutes-per-game. A real rotation supplies 240; a figure well above that (shown in amber) is an oversized off-season roster the model scales back down.

Click any team to add or remove players and watch the projection move. The recomputation is exact, not an approximation: team strength is a minute-weighted mean of player impact, so removing a player reallocates his minutes and reprices the team the same way the model does. Minutes freed below the 240-per-game budget are charged at replacement level ( per 100 possessions).
