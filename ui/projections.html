Bottom-up roster model · snapshot

NBA projected records

Built from each team's current roster: every player's impact per 100 possessions is projected from his own history with age curves applied, weighted by expected minutes, then the season is simulated game by game on a real schedule. A final adjustment carries forward part of last season's projection error for each team, since that error tends to persist about one year.

Read the ranges, not the numbers. This model's average miss in backtesting is wins, against for sportsbook preseason lines — so it does not beat the market on aggregate accuracy, and the point estimates deserve less trust than the intervals. Bars show the 80% and 50% ranges; those are calibrated (a nominal 80% range contained the true result 79.6% of the time). Ranges widen with roster turnover, which measurably predicts larger errors.
On the what-if tool. Roster edits are computed the same way the model computes a team, but the model was validated on whole rosters, not on single-player swaps. The impact-to-rating slope is fitted to map a team's aggregate talent onto its rating, and it absorbs some compression in the underlying player metric, so one-player counterfactuals extrapolate it further than the backtest checked. Treat the direction and rough scale as meaningful and the exact number as indicative. When you remove a player his minutes are offered to teammates who can cover his position, capped by how far each can stretch beyond his established role — so a deep roster absorbs the loss far better than a thin one, and only what the bench cannot cover falls to replacement level.
