Bottom-up roster model

NHL projected standings

Each team's projected regular-season standings points for the upcoming season, as a distribution — the mean plus a calibrated 80% interval — built bottom-up from the current roster (projected 5-on-5 xG-RAPM skaters → team goal rates → a regulation/OT/shootout season simulation) plus a one-year carryover. The bar spans the 80% interval; the solid tick is the projected mean. This is a research tool for finding market disagreements, not a betting product.

How to read this — definitions & honest caveats
proj points
Projected 82-game standings points (2 for a win, 1 for an overtime/shootout loss). The league averages ~92.
80% interval
The band the season's actual points land in ~80% of the time. It combines season luck (a fixed-talent team's points still swing, SD ~8.5) with our projection error. Backtest coverage: 0.82.
O / D
The team's projected 5-on-5 offense and defense rating (expected goals per 60 above/below average), the skater aggregate that drives goals-for / goals-against.
carry
One-year carryover: last season's over/under-performance vs the roster model persists ~40% (fitted ρ). It is what separates a roster that keeps beating its parts from one that regresses.
wins
Projected regulation + OT + shootout wins (the standings W). Different from points — the market (Kalshi) settles on wins, so that is what a market ring compares to.

Honest caveats. Over a 9-season backtest the model's points MAE (~10.5) does not beat a sharp market on aggregate — the NHL is a high-parity, high-luck league. The deliverable is per-team disagreement with a structural reason, plus calibrated intervals. Defense is the least-predictive component and is regressed hard. The projection is only as current as its roster snapshot (trades continue all season). Market lines, when shown, are strictly downstream — never an input to the model.
